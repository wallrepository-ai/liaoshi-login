Privacy note

Draft of 2026-10-12, written for the owner's review. It describes what the system does today; the owner will confirm or amend it.

What AID keeps about you

An account is an email address and a password. The password is stored only as a hash by Supabase Auth, the sign-in service the database runs on; nobody can read it back. The address is used to verify the account, to let you sign in from your AI client, and to send you a reset link if you ask for one.

When your AI client queries the database, AID logs the request: the wording of your question as the client sends it, the query it ran, the rows it returned, the time, the client program and the address it came from, and the account that made it. The owner reads this log to see what researchers ask and where the database falls short, and to keep the service working. Comments you submit through the connector are kept with the account that sent them.

Feedback from AID

The sign-up form has a box, unticked until you tick it, asking whether you would like feedback from AID: replies about comments you submit and news about the database. AID writes to you only if the box is ticked, and records when you made the choice and which version of this note you saw. You can withdraw at any time through the contact entry of the menu; no message is sent after that.

What AID does not do

Your address and your questions are not sold, shared or passed on to anyone, and are not used for advertising. The sign-in pages set one short-lived cookie during sign-in and no other; there is no tracking on these pages. The services that hold the data are Supabase (the database and sign-in), GitHub Pages (these pages) and the email service that delivers verification and reset links.

Deleting your account

Ask through the contact entry of the menu. The account, its profile and its sessions are deleted; comments you submitted stay with the database under no name, because they are corrections to a shared text.
